3. What are golden signals? Explain each one of them?
A: Golden signals are four key metrics — latency, traffic, errors, and saturation — for monitoring IT systems' health and performance. They offer a vital framework for efficient ITOps monitoring and management.
   Latency - Latency measures the time taken for a system to process a request. This includes the time from when a request is received until the response is sent back.
   Traffic - Traffic measures the volume of requests or transactions that the system is handling. It can be quantified in terms of requests per second, transactions per second, or throughput.
   Errors - Errors track the number or rate of failed requests in a system, such as HTTP 500 errors, timeouts, and other application-specific failures.
   Saturation - Saturation assesses how much of the system's resources (such as CPU, memory, disk, or network) are in use. It indicates how close the system is to reaching its capacity limits.

A: Correlating Web Performance to Business Metrics - Since RUM is collecting both web performance and business metrics (e.g., revenue, conversions), tools such a Blue Triangle's RUM correlate the two so you can better understand the patience, or "tenacity," of your users. 
   Customer Journey Tracking - Once you've had enough traffic for your data to become statistically significant, one of RUM's glowing benefits is its ability to track the customer journey. 
				You'll be able to identify the most popular paths to conversion (the "happy paths"), along with the paths that frequently lead to cart abandonment. This data, paired with session replay tools like Clicktale, give you the best insight into to where your site is failing to convert visitors. From the collected data, it is often evident whether the problem is performance one or content-related. Improving high trafficked paths is an easy and effective way to improve your conversion rate.
   Real-Time Monitoring - You could run Synthetics every minute of every day, but the data collected would never be able to replace the real-time insights that RUM can provide. The direct paths and actions of thousands of your users, all at once, is irreplaceable. These actions are authentic.    Deeper Segmentation - RUM allows you to better segment your data - down to the device type, operating system, specific location, traffic source, and more. Better segmentation means better insights. Better insights means more focused optimizations.

7. What is MTTD and MTTR?
A: MTTD (mean time to detect) is the average time it takes your team to discover an issue. This term is often used in cybersecurity when teams are focused on detecting attacks and breaches.
   MTTR (mean time to recovery) measures how quickly a system or service is restored after a failure.

Mimir: An open-source, "horizontally scalable" backend for Prometheus metrics that allows organizations to store and query years of data across multiple isolated teams or "tenants."
       Massive Scalability: Built by Grafana Labs, it can handle billions of active series. It is horizontally scalable and designed specifically to be "Prometheus that never ends."
Thanos: A set of components that transforms standard Prometheus deployments into a highly available, long-term storage solution by offloading data to cheap cloud object storage (like S3).
        Global Querying: Acts as an "overlay" for existing Prometheus servers. Its unique advantage is the ability to query across multiple clusters and store data in cheap object storage (S3/GCS) indefinitely.
Victoria Metrics: A "high-performance" alternative to Prometheus that is famous for its extreme resource efficiency, using significantly less CPU and RAM while providing massive ingestion speeds.
		  Performance & Efficiency: Known for being extremely lightweight and resource-efficient. It often requires significantly less RAM and CPU than Mimir or Thanos for the same workload.
Zipkin: One of the industry’s original distributed tracing systems, specifically designed to help developers visualize the journey of a single request through a complex web of microservices to find latency bottlenecks.
	Simplicity & Legacy: One of the original tracing tools. Its benefit is its widespread adoption and simplicity; if you have a legacy Java environment, Zipkin integrations are often the most stable and "plug-and-play."
SigNoz: A modern, open-source alternative to Datadog that provides a single UI for metrics, traces, and logs, built from the ground up to be native to the OpenTelemetry standard.
	OpenTelemetry-Native: Unlike older tools that added OTel support later, SigNoz was built for it. It uses ClickHouse for storage, making high-cardinality searches (like finding one specific user ID in millions of traces) incredibly fast.
New Relic: A comprehensive, SaaS-based "Intelligence Platform" that offers full-stack visibility, ranging from browser-side user monitoring to deep backend infrastructure and security analysis.
	   All-in-One SaaS: The biggest benefit is "Observability as Code" and the sheer breadth of its ecosystem. It offers "Golden Signal" dashboards automatically and integrates business KPIs directly with system health. 
Coralogix: A unique observability platform that uses "in-stream" analysis to process and alert on telemetry data in real-time before it is even stored, helping companies manage massive data volumes without spiraling costs.
