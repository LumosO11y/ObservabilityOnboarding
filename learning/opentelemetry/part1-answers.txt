1. Elaborate on the history before OpenTelemetry, how it was created and why?
A: Before we had sophisticated tools like OpenTelemetry, developers and operators used basic techniques to monitor their systems. 
   Two key tools were logs and metrics, However, as systems grew in complexity, 
   especially with the rise of cloud computing and microservices, logs and metrics alone were insufficient.
   Before OTEL two projects lead the observability world: OpenCensus and OpenTracing:
	* OpenTracing - set of vendor-neutral APIs and instrumentation for distributed tracing, allowing developers to monitor application performance across microservices.
	* OpenCensus - a set of open-source libraries, originally started by Google, designed to collect application metrics and distributed traces, enabling developers to monitor microservices and application performance
   It became clear to the industry that two projects (OpenTracing and OpenCensus) were serving similar purposes but with slightly different approaches and goals. 
   While OpenTracing offered a standard for tracing, OpenCensus had a broader scope, aiming to unify both tracing and metrics. 
   Many companies and contributors in the open-source community felt that having two competing standards caused fragmentation.
